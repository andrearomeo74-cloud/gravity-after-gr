
# Admissibility After General Relativity

## Scope

This repository does not propose a modification or extension of General Relativity.

Instead, it introduces a **meta-theoretical framing**:  
General Relativity is treated as a **locally maximally coherent description** under specific structural and informational constraints.

The question addressed here is not:
> “What replaces GR?”

but:
> “Under which regimes does GR remain admissible, and where does coherence break?”

---

## Gravity as structural closure, not force

Within the Congruity framework, gravity is interpreted as an expression of **structural closure across scale**, not as a primitive interaction to be quantized or replaced.

General Relativity achieves exceptional coherence by:
- aligning geometry with energy–momentum,
- enforcing local closure through diffeomorphism invariance,
- maintaining predictive stability across a wide range of regimes.

Its success is therefore **structural**, not ontological.

---

## Where tension appears

Breakdowns or tensions associated with gravity typically arise when:
- scale separation collapses (cosmology, early universe),
- information becomes globally coupled (horizons, entropy bounds),
- structural closure cannot be maintained locally (singularities),
- energy terms act as compensators for missing structure.

From this view, anomalies do not signal “wrong equations”, but **loss of admissibility** under changing constraints.

---

## Admissibility vs unification

Many approaches seek unification by introducing additional structures:
- quantized spacetime,
- new fields,
- hidden dimensions.

Congruity reframes the issue:
Unification fails not because gravity is incomplete,
but because **a single grammatical structure is being forced across incompatible regimes**.

Admissibility replaces unification as the primary criterion.

---

## Position within Congruity 7.0

This work situates gravity within the broader Congruity principle:

- Energy is a cost signal of unresolved structure,
- Geometry encodes closure, not causation,
- Laws remain valid only while proportionality holds.

General Relativity is therefore not superseded,
but **contextualized**.

It remains fully valid where its closure conditions are met,
and predictably strained where they are not.
